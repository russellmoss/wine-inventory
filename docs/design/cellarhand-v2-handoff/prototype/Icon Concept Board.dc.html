Domain icon system

Twelve winery objects

One grammar: a 24px box, 1.6px stroke, round caps and joins, no fills, no colour of their own. Each icon draws a thing in the winery or the shape of an operation — never an abstraction like a gear-for-settings or a chart-for-reports. There is no grape, no wine glass and no bottle silhouette in the set.

Rule of use: icons appear on the twelve domain destinations and on act types in a lineage or timeline. They never appear on buttons, list rows, form labels or section headings, and they never replace a text label. Applying them everywhere is what makes an icon set stop working.

Work orders
The paper order sheet

A ruled sheet with the last line ticked. Universal, and it matches the printed sheet that still gets clipped to the tank.

Cellar & vessels
A tank with a level line

Domed top, straight sides, and a horizontal line at the fill level. The level line is the whole point: a vessel is defined by how full it is.

Vineyard
Three trellis posts on a row line

Posts with cordon wires, taller in the middle for depth. Reads as planted rows, not as fruit — the vineyard domain is about land and blocks, not grapes.

Harvest & intake
A picking bin being filled

The tapered half-tonne bin every crush pad has, with fruit going in from above. Least universal of the set — a new seasonal worker may need the label once. It carries a text label everywhere, so once is enough.

Fermentation
An open-top with CO₂ rising

Deliberately the cellar icon minus its lid, plus bubbles and rising marks. Same object, active state — so the pair reads as one family, not two ideas.

Blending & transfers
Two streams joining, flowing down

A confluence with a direction arrow. Reversed (one stream becoming two) it is the split marker used in the lineage view — the same glyph carries both operations.

Bottling & packaging
A divided case, top handles

The case, not the bottle. Packaging is measured in cases and pallets, and a case divider is instantly legible at 20px where a bottle outline turns to mush.

Inventory & finished goods
A stacked pallet

Three cases stacked two-and-one. Distinguished from the bottling case by having no dividers and by being a group of objects.

Compliance
A filed form with a seal

A folded-corner form carrying a struck seal. The seal is what separates it from Work orders: this sheet is filed with a government, not clipped to a tank.

Accounting
Two columns between rules

A ledger's double rule with two unequal columns — the visual of a balance, not a bar chart. Reports use no icon at all.

Sales & shipments
Goods leaving the bond

A closed store with wine moving out to the right. Directional, so it can never be confused with Inventory, which is static.

Administration
Two set values on two tracks

The one deliberately generic icon in the set. Setup is genuinely a software concept, and pretending otherwise with a winery metaphor would be decoration.

Lots
A volume with three visible faces

A lot isn't a place or a container — it's a quantity of wine with an identity. A solid seen in three dimensions says "a body of liquid we track" without naming a vessel.
