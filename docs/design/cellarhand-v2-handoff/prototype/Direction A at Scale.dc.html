Direction A · at scale · v2

Warm at eight thousand barrels

A's warmth comes from cream paper, the serif headline, hairline rules and one sparing accent — none of it depends on whitespace, so all of it survives compression. What changes at scale is the unit of display, and — more importantly — the unit of measurement.

Nobody browses 8,000 barrels.

A barrel is a leaf, not a list item — reached by scanning it, searching it, or being walked to it. What you browse are lots, barrel groups, racks and tanks: roughly 130 objects.

And nobody measures a top-up.

So the app stops asking. The keg is what gets measured; barrels get a tick. When the keg empties, its volume divides across the barrels it served — recorded as an estimate, and labelled as one.

The condition this comes with

A rail of icons with no text is icon-only navigation, which this design system otherwise prohibits and which the audit flagged. Four rules make it legitimate rather than a regression:

  1. Expanded is the default. A new or seasonal user never meets the rail unless they choose it. The preference is per user and per device, and it persists.
  2. The label is always the accessible name. Every rail item carries aria-label="Work orders" whether or not a tooltip is visible. A screen reader hears the same nav in both states.
  3. Tooltips answer to focus, not just hover. Keyboard tabbing shows them; they are role="tooltip" wired with aria-describedby, never the name itself.
  4. Desktop only. Below 1024px there is no rail and no hamburger — the four labelled bottom tabs stand. Collapsing is a power-user affordance on a mouse, not a mobile pattern.

Why it earns its place: at 1280px the sidebar is 18% of the screen, and the tank board and capture grids are the densest things in the product. 172px is a column of tanks or two columns of barrel data. That is a real trade a manager should be allowed to make — and reverse in one click.

The rules that let A scale

1 · Measure what's actually measured

Kegs are measured; barrels are ticked. Derived volumes are stored with their divisor and always badged estimated, never mixed with weighed or metered numbers.

2 · The row is a group, not an item

Lots, barrel groups, halls and rounds are rows. A barrel opens when you expand to it or scan it. 8,142 barrels become ~130 browsable objects.

3 · Nominal capacity is a label, not a limit

Barrels evaporate, so a 225 L barrel always has room. Topping is never refused; an implausible estimate (over 15% of nominal) gets a warning the user can override with a reason.

4 · Name the wine, not just the vessel

Every tank tile, group row and barrel header carries its lot code, and every board is searchable by lot. "Where is the Syrah" is a first-class question.

5 · Tags carry identity

QR and NFC on barrels, groups, tanks and kegs. Scanning sets context anywhere in the app, which is the only realistic way to reach one barrel out of eight thousand.

6 · Chrome keeps its air; data compresses

Page header, breadcrumb, serif headline and the "what needs attention" sentence are unchanged from A. Rows drop to 38–40px with tabular figures. Warmth lives in the chrome.

New from engineering beyond plain A: a barrel-group entity with its own settings; a topping-keg entity with fills, a served-barrel set and a close-out that fans out estimated additions; a measured/estimated flag on ledger lines; QR + NFC capture; virtualised tables and server-side narrowing. The visual system needs nothing new.
